*Dristy* is Bengali for sight. It's open firmware and a host API for a Kendryte K210 camera module (a DFRobot SEN0305) that does vision on the camera itself, so a robot gets a compact answer over a serial line ("target at error_x, error_y") instead of a video stream it has to decode.

A robot doesn't *see*. It has a sensor that turns light into numbers, and a program that has to decide what those numbers mean before the motors move. Dristy is that program, running on the sensor.

<Fig src="/media/dristy/pipeline.webp" alt="Five boxes: light, pixels, look, answer, robot; from the lens to a few bytes on a UART" caption="From light to a number a robot can use. The last box receives four bytes, not a frame." wide />

## Why put the brain in the camera

A flight controller or a Raspberry Pi *can* run vision. Usually you shouldn't make it. If the main computer does vision, it has to ingest JPEG or YUV at tens of frames a second, and a stall in OpenCV stalls attitude control: you end up debugging why your PID is late. If the camera does vision, it sends a few bytes (an id, x, y, a size), and the robot's loop stays boring and fast. Dristy is the same idea as a GPS module: a specialist that speaks a short protocol.

It's also the same idea as Mongla's board split, at a smaller scale: keep the slow, heavy thinking away from the thing that has to be on time.

The same firmware also reads QR codes, AprilTags and ArUco markers, follows a line by regression on a contrast strip, estimates optical flow, detects faces on the KPU, and runs a hybrid detect-and-track mode for a lock that survives a missed frame.

| job | mode | measured on the desk |
|---|---|---|
| VOC detect | `DETECT` | about 7 FPS when the net runs |
| colour | `COLOUR_TRACK` | 16 blobs, about 19 FPS |
| line | `LINE_FOLLOW` | line valid, about 20 FPS |
| motion | `MOTION_DETECT` | 55 %, then 99 in the shot above |
| tags, QR, face | those modes | handshake ok; zero hits until shown a marker or face |

Zeros there mean the scene had no tag or face in it, not that the mode is dead. The audit says which.

## The budget is the architecture

The K210 has two 64-bit RISC-V cores, a convolution engine (the KPU), 8 MiB of SRAM, no Linux and no MMU. On a chip that small, where every buffer goes is the design.

<Fig src="/media/dristy/k210-budget.webp" alt="K210 SRAM budget: SRAM0 4 MiB for code and classical vision, SRAM1 2 MiB for trackers and overlays, AI SRAM 2 MiB for KPU feature maps" caption="One chip, 8 MiB of SRAM, spent on the latest frame. Model input widths are multiples of 64 so the camera can DMA straight into AI SRAM without the CPU touching a pixel." wide />

One rule runs through all of it, the same one that runs through Mongla:

<Pull>Keep the newest frame, throw the rest away. Dristy is a mailbox, not a queue.</Pull>

A robot that steers on a picture from 200 ms ago is worse off than one that missed a frame. The camera preview and the neural net even share one camera lease, so opening the detector has to stop the preview first. That isn't a bug; it's the physics of one pipe.
