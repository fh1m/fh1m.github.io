## The vision years

The camera gives the vehicle observations of task props; it is not the whole navigation system. Attitude, pressure and, on the main vehicle, a Doppler Velocity Log contribute different information. A detector can identify the gate without telling us the vehicle's complete pose. A stable depth reading cannot tell us whether we chose the right side of that gate.

My work rebuilt perception around CNN detectors, a custom dataset pipeline, domain-specific pretraining and source-free domain adaptation. I deployed inference on edge hardware and built the development and deployment tooling around it. Training, moving a model onto the vehicle and making its outputs useful to another subsystem were connected responsibilities—not one model-training step.

The first challenge was deciding what a useful example looked like. Adjacent frames from one pool video are highly correlated. A random image split can let nearly identical backgrounds, lighting and viewpoints appear on both sides of an evaluation. Holding out a recording session or a water condition asks a harder question: has the model learned the task object, or has it learned that particular afternoon? That distinction is why the [interview-bottle retrospective](/notes/interview-bottle/) keeps the original training history beside the later evaluation.

Underwater domain shift is not just a blue filter. Wavelength-dependent attenuation changes colour with distance; scattering lowers contrast and adds a veil; suspended particles, reflections and occlusion change the evidence from frame to frame. Enhancing the image can help a human inspect it while also changing the distribution seen by the detector. The useful question is not “does this frame look cleaner?” but “does the complete perception-to-action path behave better on footage it did not train on?”

## The detector is only one stage

The 2025 technical design report names YOLOv11 for Duburi 4.2. The supplied 2026 report describes YOLO26 on the Jetson Orin Nano and ByteTrack for maintaining identities across frames. Those are campaign-specific choices, not interchangeable labels for every screenshot in this archive.

A detection supplies a class, a box and a score for one observation. Tracking tries to associate that observation with an existing identity. It does not turn confidence into certainty. A track predicted through an occlusion is not a fresh detection, and a high-scoring box can still refer to the wrong object. The consumer needs to know what the output represents and how old it is.

For visual alignment, a simple starting error is the difference between the target centre and image centre. That is measured in pixels. Turning it into a yaw, lateral or depth request requires camera geometry, a sign convention and controller tuning. Apparent box size can be a distance cue only under assumptions about the object and view; a monocular model's relative depth is not automatically a metric range sensor. Putting “metres” beside an arbitrary scale does not calibrate it.

This was also a tooling problem. An operator needs to see the selected target, its error, the requested correction and the vehicle's state together. Otherwise a correct detector and an incorrect command sign can produce a very convincing dashboard while the vehicle moves the wrong way.

## A dual-camera prototype, read more carefully

The archive includes a front/down experiment and its <code>pid_v13.py</code> implementation. It combines learned detection with classical image cues. The heading panel is more specific—and more limited—than the phrase “two detectors fused into one heading” suggests.

<Fig src="/media/lab/dualcam-fusion.webp" alt="Front and downward camera views alongside a fused-heading display" caption="The archived dual-camera prototype. The heading readout is a visualization of image-derived cues, not proof of a calibrated navigation estimate or a completed autonomous run." wide contain />

In that file, the front cue is the negative dominant line angle found in the image. Its weight is based on the number of detected line segments. The downward cue comes from Farneback optical flow: select motion above the 75th-percentile magnitude, average the selected vectors, then take their direction. Its weight compares their average magnitude with the frame's maximum. These are **heuristics**, not calibrated covariance estimates.

The fusion panel converts the candidate angles into yaw-only quaternions and interpolates them, using the relative weights. Quaternions avoid the obvious problem of interpolating directly from +179° to −179° through zero. They cannot make two physically different quantities equivalent: a line's orientation, the direction of apparent ground motion and the vehicle's absolute yaw need a defined frame relationship and an observation model.

There is a useful concrete edge case in the preserved code. Before quaternion interpolation, it takes an arithmetic mean of the last five downward angles. I ran the original quaternion helper methods in isolation, without loading cameras, model weights or vehicle interfaces:

| Calculation | Result |
|---|---|
| Archived quaternion midpoint of +179° and −179° | 180° |
| Arithmetic mean of those same two downward samples | 0° |
| Equal quaternion blend of front +179° with that already-averaged 0° | 89.5° |

The interpolation handles wrapping; the earlier average has already destroyed the information. A circular mean would preserve the direction for this example, but that alone would not calibrate the cameras or validate the estimate. The example is an offline numerical probe of an archived prototype, not a measured pool failure.

The function draws the resulting heading in its panel. The separate <code>compute_control_signals</code> path computes corrections from target-centre error and either a depth output or apparent target size; the displayed fused heading is not used there. Keeping that distinction visible is better than retroactively presenting every promising experiment as a deployed controller.

## From one vehicle to two

In 2026 the programme paired Duburi 4.5 with Dubomini 2.0. The main vehicle carried the richer sensor and actuator package for precision work; the smaller vehicle was assigned manoeuvre-dominant tasks. The report describes a shared ROS 2 codebase with platform-specific configuration, and an inter-vehicle release signal represented as a mission-state transition.

The architectural split is worth explaining. The Pixhawk/ArduSub side owns stabilization, depth hold and thrust allocation. The Jetson/ROS 2 side owns higher-level perception and mission requests. The mission planner should be able to change task order without rewriting the stabilizer. Likewise, a detector should not have to know the full competition sequence to publish a usable target.

The two-vehicle handoff adds another dependency: what happens if the release message never arrives? The 2026 report specifies a bounded wait and a fallback transition for Dubomini. That is the design described in the report, not a claim that every possible acoustic-channel failure was eliminated. A shared stack reduces duplicated software; it does not erase the different sensor capabilities or failure modes of the two vehicles.

As engineering lead, the scope was wider than the vision model. Subsystems had to agree on what commands and observations meant, and the team needed to diagnose the machine as an integrated system. The hull, power distribution, payload mechanisms and other members' implementations remain team work. My role does not turn their contributions into mine.

## Research, and what can be concluded from it

I am a co-author of *[Color is Also Class Evidence: Re-evaluating Domain Generalization for Underwater Object Detection](https://openreview.net/forum?id=qiFAySNAsW)*, listed in my CV as an OpenReview 2025 publication. The question connects directly to this work: removing an underwater colour shift may also remove information that distinguishes the task classes.

That is not a universal argument against colour correction. It is a reason to evaluate the preprocessing, training distribution and class definitions together. I keep the publication record separate from the later campaign footage and later [Mongla experiments](/notes/mongla/); a year on a publication entry does not establish that it evaluated a subsequent year's competition.

For the implementation history, the [2025 team technical report](https://robonation.org/app/uploads/sites/4/2025/07/RS25_TDR_BRAC-University-Duburi_compressed.pdf) provides the vehicle architecture and planned task sequence. The supplied 2026 report provides the later platform split. A technical design report records a team's design and testing account; the competition score sheets answer the narrower question of what earned points in the run.

Neither a tracking algorithm nor a middleware choice validates a vehicle by itself. That proof has to survive the timing, calibration and conditions of the machine using it.
