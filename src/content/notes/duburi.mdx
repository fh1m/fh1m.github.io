BRACU Duburi was founded at BRAC University in 2017: Bangladesh's autonomous underwater vehicle team. I joined its vision sub-team in September 2023, and two months later had written forty lines of OpenCV that [found green](/notes/green-at-depth). I left in 2026 as engineering team lead, with three RoboSub participation certificates in a folder and a much lower opinion of colour thresholds.

## Rebuilt, not patched

The vehicle line runs from 1.0 to 4.5, plus a mini-AUV, Dubomini. Every generation re-opened the architecture instead of bolting onto the last one, which is the instinct Jim Keller describes for chips: every few years, design it again rather than patch it. Mechanical, electrical and software sub-teams move together, because the machine is one system and the water doesn't care which sub-team a problem belongs to.

## The vision years

Underwater, everything Duburi does (find the gate, align, fire, surface) it decides from the inside, from a camera, an IMU and pressure. So vision isn't a feature; it's the vehicle's whole idea of where it is.

I rebuilt the team's perception on modern CNN detectors with a custom dataset pipeline, then added domain-specific pretraining and source-free domain adaptation, so a model trained in one pool would survive water it had never seen. Then it went onto edge hardware on the vehicle, and everything got wrapped in tooling so the team could train, deploy and debug without me in the loop. That last part mattered most. A capability one person can operate is a capability with a bus factor of one.

<Fig src="/media/lab/mission-control.webp" alt="The mission control HUD: gate detected, alignment error, depth and mode on one screen" caption="Mission control: the gate-alignment HUD. Detection, error, depth and mode on one screen." wide contain />

Two cameras beat one. Instead of choosing which camera to trust, both run: a forward gate detector and a downward bin detector, each with its own controller, fused into one heading by how much each should be believed right now.

<Fig src="/media/lab/dualcam-fusion.webp" alt="Two camera views, forward and down, with detections and a fused heading readout" caption="Front + down, fused: heading +94.0° with weights 0.36 and 0.49. When you can't pick a threshold, run both and let the consumer decide." wide contain />

The research side of this became a paper with the team: *Color is Also Class Evidence: Re-evaluating Domain Generalization for Underwater Object Detection* (OpenReview, 2025), tested against our RoboSub 2025 and 2026 footage. Its argument is in the title. The standard move is to treat underwater colour shift as noise and normalise it away. Sometimes the colour *is* the class.

Same placing both years, 53 % more points on the best round. I led the 2025 vision sub-team and the 2026 engineering. Both years we finished short of the finals wild-card line. That gap, and a 20 Hz control loop steering a box we weren't allowed inside, are what [Mongla](/notes/mongla) was built to answer.

In 2026 I left the university, on principle. The vehicles, the team and the name stayed with the programme, where they belong. What I took was what the water taught.
