Real detectors don't fire every frame. Underwater, against murk and sun caustics, they drop out for stretches. If the controller only acts when the detector fires, the vehicle stutters and loses its target.

We counted the gaps. Not guessed: counted, at native frame rate, over 300 consecutive frames × 3 windows × 5 clips. **71 real gaps.**

## A ladder, not a timeout

So you build a *lock ladder*: a small state machine that coasts a target through detection gaps, one rung at a time, each rung's timeout sized against the real distribution of gaps rather than a guessed constant.

One clip held its target through a gap of **175 frames** with no detection at all. The vehicle still knew where the target was, because an anchor matched image structure instead of asking the network again.

## Asymmetric failure sets the gates

The rungs are honest about confidence. The ladder ends in a refusal, LOST, not in a confident invention.

<Pull>A rung that names the wrong prop confidently is worse than one that reports LOST, because LOST is honest.</Pull>

The failure modes are asymmetric. A missed re-acquisition costs a second. A false one costs the run. You'd rather lose the target and know it than weld two objects into one identity and have every downstream consumer believe the target teleported. Robustness here isn't a bigger model. It's refusing to lie during the gap.

The ladder's limits have their own note: [179 identity switches](/notes/179-identity-switches), and why the state-of-the-art fix costs more than this vehicle can pay.
