Mongla estimates its own position with a right-invariant extended Kalman filter. It predicts forward on the board's IMU, and corrects with whatever cheap measurement arrives: depth from pressure, [velocity from the floor](/notes/turning-a-camera-into-a-sensor), heading, and occasional position fixes from recognising a prop.

That last kind of correction has a problem nobody writes on the box. A camera exposes a frame, the Hailo-8 finds the prop, the result crosses two processes and a queue, and it lands in the filter. By then the vehicle has moved. The fix is perfectly correct about where the vehicle *was*.

## The chord

If the vehicle is going straight at speed *v*, a fix that's τ seconds old pulls the estimate straight back along the path by *v·τ*. In a turn at rate ω it's worse: the old position sits on an arc behind you, and the correction yanks the estimate along the chord <code>2(v/ω)·sin(ωτ/2)</code>, back *and* sideways. Set the speed to zero and both tracks agree exactly. Latency times zero speed is zero error. That's why the bug survives every bench test, every dry run and every "we checked it in the lab": the condition that reveals it (moving, turning, and being corrected at the same time) is the one you can't create indoors.

<Pull>A late measurement is not noise. It's a true statement, applied to the wrong moment.</Pull>

## Putting it where it belongs

The filter keeps a short buffer of its own recent states. When a measurement arrives carrying the instant it describes, the filter winds back to that instant, applies the correction there, and replays every motion input since. The vehicle's present position becomes a consequence of the correction rather than an override.

That only works if the timestamp is real. A message that says *the prop is here* is a rumour. One that says *the prop was here, at this instant on the board's clock* is a measurement. The board stamps its own messages, which is what makes the rewind possible at all.

## Why invariant

A vehicle's orientation lives on a curved space: rotations don't add like numbers. An ordinary EKF linearises around its current guess, and after a fast turn it can become confidently wrong, which is the worst way to be wrong. The invariant form keeps the filter's error behaviour independent of where the vehicle happens to be pointing. In practice that means it doesn't get overconfident after a turn, which is exactly when the late fixes above do their damage.

## The DVL question

Does Mongla need the expensive sensor? A spec sheet can't answer that. Only whether the cheap estimate clears the bar the mission needs, measured in water, can. Until then the honest state is written down: the board can't yet even accept an external velocity measurement, so a 1.09 cm-good estimate currently has nowhere to go. It's on the roadmap as a pull request, not hidden.
