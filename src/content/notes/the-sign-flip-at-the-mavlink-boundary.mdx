The largest unresolved warning in the project's own documentation read: *the depth loop has never run closed.* Every mission primitive closes it implicitly, even a plain forward move. And an in-air test proves nothing, because at roughly zero metres the target and the measurement already agree by default.

The only real test was a dynamics plant with actual feedback: the first time the depth-control firmware ever ran against anything but open air.

## Two correct conventions, read together, wrong

Three attempts flew the simulated hull to **+20 metres** (above the water, in a pool 1.6 m deep) before the cause surfaced.

Inside the firmware, depth is positive metres *down*: the setpoint is clamped non-negative, and a SURFACE command sets it to zero. But the telemetry channel reports depth as negative *below* the surface. Both internally correct. Passing one straight into a caller that expected the other read as *the vehicle is above the water*, to a controller that then tried hard to make that true.

<Pull>Every boundary between two correct pieces of code is a place to write down which way is up.</Pull>

## Fixed

The integral term earns its place. A real hull is never exactly neutrally buoyant, and against ±2 N of standing buoyancy error the loop still settled at 1.500 m with 0.000 m measured error. P alone would have parked short forever, pushing exactly hard enough to hold and not hard enough to close the gap. You can watch that happen in the [gimbal bench](/#air) with the wind on.

Overshoot behaves like a physical system should, recorded as numbers rather than shapes: target 0.5 m gave 0.202 m of overshoot at a peak descent of 0.42 m/s; target 1.0 m gave 0.307 m.

<Fig src="/media/mongla/pid-lab.webp" alt="The Mongla site's PID panel: a response curve with overshoot and the three gain controls" caption="The control-loop explainer on the Mongla site: P, I, D, a current that pushes sideways, and the loop rate." wide contain />

It's still a simulation. The project's roadmap keeps *the board's automatic mode has never held depth in water* as its first open item, with two armed bench checks gating every automatic move until it has.
