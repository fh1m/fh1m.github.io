December 2023. The BRACU Duburi team gave applicants a task: train an object detector. I took a Kaggle water-bottle dataset, labelled it by hand in LabelImg, fine-tuned **SSD MobileNet v2 320×320** with the TensorFlow Object Detection API, and sent in screenshots like this one.

<Fig src="/media/notes/interview-bottle-2023-shot.webp" alt="2023 screenshot: the detector boxing a Winner water bottle at 0.88 confidence, the box much wider than the bottle" caption="<b>December 2023.</b> Bottle: 0.88. Very pleased. Look how much wider than the bottle that box is. That's not an accident; it's the subject of this note." />

Three years later the 2023 workspace was still on disk, complete with TensorBoard logs, the exported model and the TFRecords. So I re-opened the box.

## What the logs remember

<Fig src="/media/notes/interview-bottle-training_2023.webp" alt="Training losses and learning rate read back from the 2023 event files: learning rate warms up to 0.8; regularisation loss jumps and stays high" caption="Read back from the 2023 event files. The learning rate climbs to 0.8, and the regularisation loss jumps with it." wide plot />

- **15 training images.** Ten for validation. That's the whole dataset.
- **4,200 of 50,000 steps.** The run was stopped about 8 % of the way in.
- **A learning rate meant for someone else's computer.** The config came from the model zoo, where `learning_rate_base: 0.8` is tuned for a **batch of 512 on TPUs**. Mine was **8**. The linear scaling rule says it should have been about 0.8 × 8 / 512 ≈ **0.0125**. The weights were being shoved around by steps 64 times too big.

## The grade

The re-grade's first finding came before any model ran:

<Pull>test.tfrecord is a byte-for-byte copy of train.tfrecord.</Pull>

All 15 "test" images are training images. On them, the model scores AP@0.5 = 1.000. Memory, not skill. The honest held-out set is the 10 validation images:

| what | AP@0.5 |
|---|---:|
| "test" split (= training images) | 1.000 |
| **validation, never trained on** | **0.405** |
| logged by the 2023 eval job (validation, step 4000) | 0.506 |

Why so low, when the boxes *look* right? A habit:

<Stats items={[
  { v: "72 %", l: "median share of the frame covered by the model's top box", tone: "failure" },

  { v: "51 %", l: "covered by the real bottle, training images" },

Every training image is one product-shot bottle, front and centre, filling half the frame. Fifteen times. The cheapest correct answer the network could learn was *a big box in the middle*, and it learned that very well.

## Would it have survived the pool?

This was an interview for an *underwater* team, so the last question is obvious. The validation images go through the same water model as [green, at depth](/notes/green-at-depth).

## What I'd change, and haven't

The repo has a `pipeline.fixed.config` with the fixes this argues for: the learning rate scaled to the batch, a short warm-up, a real held-out split, and augmentations that break the habit (bottles small and off-centre; hue and brightness jitter closer to water). **It has not been retrained**, and the note says so in bold, because every number above is from the 2023 model.

The 2023 repo also carried about 170 MB of other people's code: the model garden, cocoapi, protoc and the zoo checkpoint. That's upstream's to host. The repo is lighter now, and honest about what's mine.
