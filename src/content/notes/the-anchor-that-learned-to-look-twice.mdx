The guard read, almost apologetically, `not self._anchor.has_reference`. The anchor took its reference image once, the first time it saw the target, and never refreshed it. Measured against archive clips, inliers against that one frozen photo told the whole story.

Run the same frames against a *fresh* reference and they score what the +1 s column scored. Nothing about the descriptor changed. What changed was refusing to take a second photograph.

## Composing, not replacing

The fix, `CheckpointBank`, holds up to five references over one backend instead of one photo forever. It composes `Anchor` rather than replacing it, so the ROI scaling, the homography, the pose estimate and the below-bar refusal all stay code that had already been measured on real footage. The bank adds only what one frozen photo structurally can't: which reference answered, which to discard, and when the evidence says it's time for a new one.

<Pull>A table of inlier counts says a match is good. It never says good at what.</Pull>

## Three defects no table showed

Shipping the bank surfaced three more defects, found by a tool that draws what the system actually believes (the matched quadrilateral, the surviving correspondences, the warped annotations, the identity verdict and its reason) on real footage. Looking at it for a few minutes found them.

The sharpest: on close-range frames with the target filling the screen, the system refused every one at 7–8 inliers. A confidence floor of 0.60 was rejecting candidate views that the detector itself only scores at a median of 0.49 on this footage.

A fixed floor was the wrong shape of rule. It came down to 0.25, and the checks that actually discriminate (geometric agreement, eviction by yield, a 40-inlier recognition bar) were left to do the job the threshold had been standing in for.
