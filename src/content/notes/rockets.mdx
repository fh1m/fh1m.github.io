Before the AUV there was a rocket. In February 2023 I joined Amateur Experimental Rocketry Dhaka (AERD), founded in 2022 and backed by AIUB's research centre. I started where a machine has the fewest places to hide: something that has to leave the ground and come back in a recoverable number of pieces.

My jobs were attitude, guidance, navigation and control, trajectory prediction, the parachute, and procurement. Procurement isn't glamorous, but it decides which design you actually get to build.

## What I built

- **A ROS 2 (Humble) data-acquisition stack**, so a test produced data instead of anecdotes.
- **Flight controllers** (Pixhawk, Betaflight), and V1 of an in-house flight computer for a monocopter UAV, which I led.
- **A visual SLAM stack for GPS-denied navigation.** Like underwater, there are times there's nothing overhead you can trust.
- **Trajectory prediction with RocketPy, checked against real flight-test data.** A simulation you never compare to a flight is a wish.

The engine fired at AIUB's research facility: the first hybrid rocket engine tested in Bangladesh, covered by The Daily Star and others. In 2023 AIUB and AERD also launched their first weather balloon. I was one member of the team behind both, and I'd rather be precise about that than impressive.

## The gimbal

From September 2024, at BRAC University's LASSET lab, I worked controls and trajectory for the university's rocketry sub-team. I built an in-house PID controller for a thrust-vector-control gimbal, a 2-axis controller, and a GUI to track and monitor it. The demo went to the Research Excellence Awards Initiative as a funding submission.

A thrust-vector gimbal holds a rocket upright by tilting the engine under it, like balancing a broom on a fingertip. Everything you need to know about PID is in that broom.

- **P** pushes in proportion to the tilt. Alone, it overshoots and rings: when the rocket is finally upright, it's still rotating.
- **D** pushes back against how fast the tilt is changing. It's the brake. It also amplifies sensor noise, which is why nobody turns it up to eleven.
- **I** remembers the error that's still there. Only I beats a steady wind, and only I winds up and takes over if you let it.

You can tune the gimbal yourself in [the air arena](/#air): press *P only*, then *add D*, then *add I*, and watch the three classic failures go by in order. It's a toy plant with honest shapes, not our rocket's numbers.
