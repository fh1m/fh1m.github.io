Leaving a board alone is a useful experiment because the intended motion is simple. It does not make its absolute position known, independently calibrate gravity, or prove zero motion from an accelerometer's magnitude alone.

The primary ledger reports a disarmed SROT board on a bench on **24 September 2026**, with the following analysed IMU record. The complete thirty-minute recording is not available in this site's published data, so these are attributed report values—not statistics I have independently recomputed from that full recording.

A quiet magnitude trace is a useful consistency check. It is not a complete motion detector: steady translation produces no additional acceleration, and rotation about gravity can preserve the magnitude. “The bench was untouched” remains a statement about the experimental setup, not a theorem implied by one standard deviation.

## Twenty seconds you can inspect

The page does carry **1,000 accelerometer rows from a different session**, dated 15 September. Its provenance explicitly says it predates a frame-convention fix: orientation was north-east-down (NED), gyro forward-right-down (FRD) and acceleration still in the sensor's unmapped axes. Those are not interchangeable coordinate labels.

<Explore id="imu" n="fig 1" title="A board at rest, twenty real seconds" hint="drag the two handles" caption="A separate accelerometer extract from board_still_2026-09-15.json. The first-to-last span is 19.98075 s; the median timestamp step is 20 ms. It is not the thirty-minute gyro recording." />

Recomputing all the published rows gives an acceleration-magnitude mean of **9.71722 m/s²** and a sample standard deviation of **0.00526 m/s²**. The selector above computes statistics for the chosen window. Neither should silently be substituted for the longer record's numbers.

The unequal axis means are compatible with a tilted mounting and projected gravity, but a single pose does not uniquely separate tilt, scale and accelerometer offset. Check the frame and mounting before calling a non-zero axis a new bias.

## The headline

The longer record reports these gyro means and per-sample scatter:

| Axis | Mean (°/s) | Scatter (°/s) |
|---|---:|---:|
| x | −0.000131 | 0.0888 |
| y | +0.000321 | 0.0994 |
| z | −0.000018 | 0.0987 |

The means are small relative to individual-sample scatter in this one stationary session. The report describes these as rates **after the board's own bias correction**. They are not an independent calibration of the uncorrected gyro across temperature, motion and future sessions.

There are two different mistakes to avoid. Subtracting the same calibrated offset twice is wrong. But it does not follow that every estimator must forbid a residual bias state: that depends on which signal it receives, its measurement model, observability and the residual errors being estimated. Small means in one run do not settle that architectural choice.

## Allan deviation asks a different question

A per-sample standard deviation describes scatter at that sampling scale. Allan deviation compares averages over adjacent intervals of duration **τ**, then asks how the difference changes as that interval grows:

```text
d = mean(next block) - mean(previous block)
Allan deviation = sqrt(mean(d²) / 2)
```

For independent white rate noise, averaging produces the familiar falling trend proportional to **τ⁻¹ᐟ²**. Correlated noise and slower processes change the shape. A minimum in a finite computed curve is not automatically a well-supported bias-instability coefficient or a recommended filter averaging time.

### The long end of this record is thin evidence

The report places the smallest computed values at approximately **660 s** for x/y and **858 s** for z. The actual script increases the averaging-block size by a factor of about 1.3 and takes the minimum among the tested points. Reconstructing that grid from 89,813 samples over 1,797.0 s puts its last two times at **659.94 and 857.92 s**. The reported z minimum is the **last tested point**, not an observed turn back upward.

Only two whole, non-overlapping averaging blocks fit at either of those long intervals. Overlap lets the implementation compute many shifted differences—4,057 at its last point—but they reuse almost the same data. They are not 4,057 independent long-duration trials. [NIST's confidence discussion](https://tf.nist.gov/phase/Properties/five.htm) explains why overlapping estimates cannot simply be assigned one independent degree of freedom per difference.

The schematic curve previously shown here implied a demonstrated upturn after the minimum. That implication has been removed. Establishing the long-term noise model needs the full data, estimator conventions and confidence analysis; an attractive curve is not a substitute.

### The sample clock is part of the estimator

The script reads rosbag record timestamps and computes one interval from the total span divided by the sample intervals. Its Allan calculation then treats that interval as uniform. It does not explicitly reject timestamp gaps or reconstruct sensor acquisition times from message headers.

That is a method to inspect before treating its output as a sensor-only property. Missing samples, transport timing and an axis/frame change can affect what a record represents. [Dhara](/notes/dhara/) follows a related boundary: a numerically positive time difference can still refer to the wrong events.

## Gravity, offset and the missing depth measurement

The longer report's acceleration-x mean is **−0.8718 m/s²**. Gravity projected onto a tilted board can produce a large non-zero component. Subtracting that entire value as an offset would erase part of the observation rather than calibrate it.

The report also flags the measured magnitude as about **0.81% below standard gravity**, using 9.80665 m/s² as its reference. Standard gravity is a convention, not an independent measurement of gravity at this bench. The discrepancy deserves calibration work; it does not, by itself, identify every part of the difference as sensor scale error.

Another limitation is recorded plainly: the barometer was **not initialised** during the long session. There is no depth-noise result here. The record also describes the associated motion-mode refusals; this disarmed bench recording is not a movement trial.

## What I would carry into the next experiment

Keep the raw observations, their timestamps, temperature and firmware/frame configuration alongside derived summaries. State which corrections already happened on the board, whose board and firmware they are—Rakibul Islam's here—and which reference assumptions remain unverified.

The useful outcome is a bounded baseline for another recording and a better question for the estimator. It is not a certificate that heading will drift at the same rate during a mission, that a residual bias can never matter, or that the depth sensor was characterised when it was not.
