Every engineer has a workshop: the tools they built because the shop didn't sell the one they needed, or sold it with too many buttons. Mine mostly live in terminals and in the first thing a browser shows.

## A new tab that takes a key

A browser new-tab page: a black screen, a clock and a text box. Built in October 2023 on Ozenc Bilgili's Tilde Enhanced, then re-keyed for how I actually move around the web.

<Fig src="/media/notes/start-page-2023.webp" alt="October 2023: the start page with a large clock, on a riced tiling-window desktop" caption="<b>October 2023</b>, in its natural habitat: a riced bspwm desktop. The setup has since moved on." wide />

Every site I open more than twice a day gets a one- or two-letter key. Type the key, press Enter, done. The same keys take arguments: `g:kalman filter` searches GitHub, `r/r/robotics` opens a subreddit, `?` shows the grid, and anything else becomes a web search.

<Stats items={[

The whole brain is one parser that checks in a fixed order: does it look like a URL? Is it *exactly* a key? Is it `key:query`, then `key/path`? Otherwise search. Order matters. `d` (Drive) and `dc` (Discord) share a first letter, and that only works because a key must match exactly: prefixes never win. You can type into the real grammar in the [workshop arena](/#workshop).

## Estimating where a car is pointing

In 2023 I attempted comma.ai's open calibration challenge: given dashcam video, recover the camera's pitch and yaw relative to the direction of travel. It's a fork, an attempt, and the fun was in the estimation, not the leaderboard. It was also the first time I met the idea that motion itself is a measurement: the road slides past the camera in a pattern that points at where the car is going.
