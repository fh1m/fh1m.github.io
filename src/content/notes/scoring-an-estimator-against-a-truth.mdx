The localisation filter had 21 tests, and every one checked a *property*, not a *trajectory*: the exp/log round-trip, covariance symmetry, positive-definiteness, the sign of one update. None of them drove the filter along a path and asked how far it had drifted from where it actually was.

Because until a physics plant existed, there was no "actually" to compare against.

<Pull>Comparing two estimators only measures whether they agree. It can't rank them.</Pull>

You need a case where the true position is known by construction.

## Build the truth first

The plant is that case. It's a Fossen-model rigid body: the standard equations of motion for a marine vehicle, with added mass, damping and restoring forces. It produces exact position, attitude and velocity every tick. So the test harness can synthesise what the sensors would have said (IMU readings, optical-flow velocity, depth, heading) at their real measured rates, then score the filter's drift against ground truth instead of against its own earlier output.

## The frame seam that cost −1,988 degrees

One convention had to be pinned rather than assumed, because getting it wrong once had already produced a spectacular failure. The filter's world is NED, z down, with a forward-right-down body frame. The plant is x forward, y starboard, z down. Same convention, nothing to remap. That sentence is worth writing down, because a frame seam is exactly what produced a **−1,988° yaw runaway** the first time the thruster allocator met the plant with an unpinned assumption about which frame it was reading.

Nearly six full turns, confidently, in the wrong direction. The vehicle wasn't confused. It was certain.

## The first numbers that mean what they say

Baseline: 60 seconds of a yawing trajectory with full aiding.

Those three numbers aren't impressive in isolation. They matter because they're the first in the project's history that mean what they appear to mean: an estimate compared with a truth, on a path complex enough that agreement wasn't guaranteed by construction.
