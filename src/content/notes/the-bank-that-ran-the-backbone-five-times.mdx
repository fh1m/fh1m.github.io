The number that should have moved didn't. A recognition lookup cost 42.3 ms at `top_k = 1024`, 36.3 ms at 512, 13.3 ms at 256. Halving the keypoints from 1024 to 512 cut the multiply-accumulates in the matcher by about three quarters, and the time by 14 %.

## Five passes over the same pixels

`Anchor.locate()` detects features internally. That's fine for one reference photograph. But `CheckpointBank.locate()` asks several references about the *same* frame, and each of them called the full detector on identical pixels. A four-wide shortlist ran the neural backbone four times on one image, plus once more for the signature path: **five backbone passes per lookup, four of them recomputing something already in memory.**

## The fix is smaller than the bug

`Anchor.locate_features(k1, d1)` now accepts features that were already extracted. The bank detects once and passes them to every reference. At the shipping width, the lookup went from **169.1 ms to 94.4 ms**, 1.8 times faster, at zero accuracy cost, because nothing was approximated.

<Bars title="one bank lookup at the shipping width" unit=" ms" rows={[
  { label: "five backbone passes", v: 169.1, tone: "failure" },
  { label: "one pass, shared", v: 94.4, tone: "measured" },
]} />

It also overturned an earlier conclusion. A previous section had said the Hailo-8 saves a fixed ~31.7 ms regardless of bank size, assuming detection happens once per lookup. It didn't: it happened *k + 1* times, so the chip's saving actually scaled with the shortlist width. Removing the redundancy on CPU alone recovered 75 ms; what's left is what moves onto the accelerator next.
